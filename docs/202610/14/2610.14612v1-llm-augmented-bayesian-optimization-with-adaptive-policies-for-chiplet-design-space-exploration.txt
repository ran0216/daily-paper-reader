## Abstract 

Chiplet technology is a key approach for extending Moore’s Law. However, its design process is constrained by two primary chal-lenges: the scarcity of early-stage platforms for rapidly evaluating chiplet-based heterogeneous systems, and the complex relation-ships between design parameters and optimization metrics. To address these challenges, we propose a synergistic framework that integrates a Large Language Model (LLM) with Bayesian Optimiza-tion (BO), enabling architectural-level simulation and automated optimization for the 2.5D/3D CPU-GPU heterogeneous chiplets. We extend the open-source chiplet simulator to evaluate 3D designs by supporting graph topologies. Further, to enhance the exploration efficiency of BO, we introduce an LLM that generates high-quality policies to guide the optimization process, enabled by our proposed adaptive directional policy reasoning (ADPR) and an exploration gain-aware fine-tuning method. Finally, we use the policy-guided BO to perform an automated and efficient exploration of the com-plex chiplet design space. Experiments on 2.5D and 3D chiplet reveal that our framework improves design space exploration efficiency. 

## 1 Introduction 

In the post-Moore era [16], where traditional scaling faces diminish-ing returns and rising costs, chiplet-based heterogeneous systems have emerged as a key paradigm to overcome monolithic SoC lim-itations [6]. This approach allows flexible integration of modular dies from diverse process nodes and functionalities, yielding su-perior performance at lower manufacturing costs. However, such high modularity and heterogeneity introduce inherent challenges in design complexity. A critical bottleneck in chiplet development is the lack of ad-equate early-stage evaluation tools for CPU-GPU heterogeneous chiplets. Existing simulators, such as HeteroGarnet [2] and Book-Sim2 [13], primarily focus on interconnect modeling and do not support the holistic simulation of 2.5D/3D heterogeneous chiplets. Therefore, we need a method that provides rapid and reliable perfor-mance evaluation early in the design cycle to guide design directions and avoid costly exploratory detours efficiently. Furthermore, the inherent heterogeneity of chiplet systems cre-ates a significant optimization challenge. Integrating various IPs from different process nodes establishes highly complex and non-linear relationships between architectural parameters and perfor-mance metrics. Traditional Design Space Exploration (DSE) meth-ods, while mathematically robust, struggle to handle this surging complexity efficiently. On one hand, chiplet-specific frameworks like RapidChiplet[12] and HISIM[25] rely on heuristic, formula-based analytical models, which do not provide DSE algorithmic optimization for automated architecture search. On the other hand,

In the context of chiplet design, where cycle-accurate simulations are computa-tionally expensive, these data-driven methods face a severe small-sample constraint. So, incorporating microarchitectural expertise is important for improving sample efficiency, especially when costly cycle-accurate simulations provide only limited data. However, ex-isting approaches often introduce such domain knowledge into DSE in a relatively rigid manner. By leveraging domain knowledge, LLMs can interpret complex architectural parameters of heterogeneous chiplets and guide op-timization in a knowledge-driven manner [4, 26]. Recent works have also started to apply LLMs to DSE. For instance, LLAMBO [17] and LEMOE [15] integrate LLMs into Bayesian optimization to accelerate the process via intelligent warm-starting, surrogate modeling, and candidate sampling. LLM-DSE [24] proposes a multi-agent framework for HLS design, ChatDSE [21] uses a zero-shot conversational approach to explore microarchitectures, and Yao et al. [29] use an LLM as both a feature extractor and an autonomous agent for HLS directive optimization. Despite their promise, these methods rely on a flawed assumption: the LLM is treated as an ora-cle that can predict the performance of any design and recommend the optimum. In practice, LLMs are unreliable for precise numeri-cal optimization and may generate biased recommendations when faced with unfamiliar chiplet designs, limiting their effectiveness and generalization. To address these challenges, we propose a framework for the ar-chitectural development and optimization of heterogeneous chiplets. We first extend the 2.5D simulator LegoSim [31] to support 3D archi-tectures by introducing a graph-based topology representation and routing. We then integrate the domain knowledge and reasoning of an LLM with the strategic exploration of Bayesian optimization, thereby improving the efficiency of DSE for heterogeneous chiplets. Our contributions are summarized as follows: 

• We propose an LLM-augmented Bayesian optimization frame-work for the optimization of heterogeneous 2.5D/3D chiplets, where LLM-generated adaptive directional policies comple-ment BO-based numerical search. 

• We introduce an adaptive policy reasoning mechanism and exploration gain-aware fine-tuning method to improve the quality of LLM-generated search policies. 

• We propose a policy-guided Bayesian optimization using a novel P-EHVI acquisition function, which leverages LLM poli-cies to focus the exploration on more promising regions. 

• We conduct experiments on both 3D and 2.5D chiplet datasets. Results demonstrate that our framework increases hypervol-ume by 13 .3% compared to the previous method on the primary 3D dataset, and achieves a 9 .3% improvement in hypervolume on the 2.5D dataset without retraining, demonstrating trans-ferability across related CPU-GPU chiplet tasks. 

## 2 Preliminaries 2.1 Chiplet-based Heterogeneous Systems 

Chiplet technology is an advanced paradigm for heterogeneous integration. This approach decomposes a conventional monolithic SoC into multiple independent chips, which enables flexible in-tegration across technology nodes and IP from different vendors and yields substantial benefits in performance, cost, and develop-ment time. Our work focuses on a representative heterogeneous 3D/2.5D chiplet system composed of a host CPU and multiple GPUs interconnected by an interposer for high-efficiency on-package communication. To perform architectural-level simulation, we use the LegoSim simulator [31], which integrates multiple standalone simulators, including Gem5[3] for CPUs and GPGPU-Sim[14] for GPUs, via a dedicated communication protocol. 

## 2.2 Adaptive Policy for DSE 

Traditional DSE methods, such as Bayesian optimization [18] and genetic algorithms [10], typically treat the design evaluator as a black box and infer promising configurations from observed sam-ples. Under limited simulation budgets, however, sparse observa-tions may be insufficient to efficiently identify beneficial parameter adjustments. In contrast, microarchitecture engineers use domain knowledge to identify performance bottlenecks and reason about the likely effects of adjusting related design parameters. Inspired by this process, we define an adaptive directional policy 

𝜋 : S → A , which maps the current exploration state 𝒔 ∈ S to a directional action 𝒂 ∈ A . The state summarizes the current Pareto set, the selected anchor design, and feedback from previous itera-tions, while each element of 𝒂 specifies whether the corresponding parameter should be increased, decreased, or maintained. Unlike fixed heuristic rules, the LLM uses Adaptive Directional Policy Rea-soning (ADPR) to regenerate the policy at each iteration based on the current exploration state and previous feedback. The resulting policy adapts to changing bottlenecks and provides a qualitative search prior that biases BO toward promising parameter adjust-ments without replacing its uncertainty-based candidate selection.

## 3 LLM-Augmented Bayesian Optimization Framework 

This section presents our LLM-augmented Bayesian optimization framework, as illustrated in Figure 2. The framework combines Adaptive Directional Policy Reasoning (ADPR), exploration gain-aware fine-tuning, and policy-guided Bayesian optimization. ADPR enables the LLM to use the current exploration state and simulation feedback to generate adaptive directional policies. The fine-tuning objective improves policy quality, while P-EHVI incorporates these policies as soft search priors into BO. 

## 3.1 Why LLM-BO Synergy? 

Chiplet DSE aims to identify Pareto designs under a limited sim-ulation budget. Traditional methods rely primarily on sampled data and usually incorporate prior knowledge through static rules or biased initialization, limiting their effectiveness in sparse-data regimes. LLMs can interpret design context and generate flexible qualitative search strategies, but they remain unreliable for precise numerical prediction and may introduce systematic search bias. We therefore combine their complementary strengths: the LLM gener-ates adaptive directional policies that bias the acquisition function toward promising regions, while BO remains the numerical opti-mization engine and balances exploitation with uncertainty-aware exploration. This division of roles improves search focus without replacing BO’s principled candidate selection. 

## 3.2 Overall Optimization Flow 

We extend LegoSim from 2.5D to 3D chiplet architectures by intro-ducing graph-based topology modeling and routing, together with a TSV power model. Based on the enhanced simulator, we construct representative 2.5D and 3D CPU-GPU chiplet design spaces and optimize three objectives: power, cycles per instruction (CPI), and interconnect delay. After each architectural simulation, the per-chiplet power estimates are mapped to the corresponding package floorplan, and HotSpot [30] estimates the peak steady-state tem-perature. Designs exceeding the temperature limit are marked as thermally infeasible and excluded from the feasible Pareto set. The optimization engine is decoupled from the architectural evaluator, allowing the framework to be extended to other target systems, such as DRAM, NPU, and CIM-based architectures, by integrating the corresponding simulators. Figure 3 shows the detailed workflow of a single optimization iteration within our synergistic DSE framework. The optimiza-tion loop proceeds as follows: first, our exploration gain-aware fine-tuned LLM generates adaptive policies for parameter optimiza-tion through engineer-like reasoning. Second, these policies guide Bayesian optimization to focus on promising subspaces, which enhances DSE efficiency and accelerates convergence. 

## 3.3 Adaptive Directional Policy Reasoning 

Directly using an LLM to predict optimal configurations is unre-liable because chiplet DSE involves complex numerical trade-offs. In practical design exploration, engineers instead follow an itera-tive process that identifies performance bottlenecks, reasons about suitable parameter adjustments, and revises the search direction according to observed outcomes. Inspired by this process, we propose Adaptive Directional Policy Reasoning (ADPR), a structured reasoning procedure that enables the LLM to generate qualitative search guidance rather than directly predict design performance. At each iteration, ADPR uses bench-mark information, the current exploration state, and feedback from the previous iteration to produce an adaptive directional policy. ## 4 Experiment 4.1 Experimental Setup 

Target Architectures and Benchmarks: As shown in Figure 1, we evaluate two heterogeneous chiplet architectures[28], both compris-ing an ARM-based host CPU and PTX-480 GPUs: a 1-CPU+3-GPU system with 3D packaging running a compute-intensive MLP infer-ence benchmark, and a 1-CPU+2-GPU system using 2.5D packaging, tasked with a parallel matrix multiplication benchmark. 

Design Space and Simulation Environment: As shown in Table 1, our experimental setup defines a heterogeneous chiplet design space with 11 parameters (6 for CPU, 2 for GPU, and 3 for interconnect), totaling 18,432 configurations. Each configuration is evaluated for power, CPU CPI, and interconnect delay using our enhanced 2.5D/3D chiplet simulation platform. From the suite of simulators integrated within the platform, we select Sniper [5] for CPU simulation and GPGPU-Sim [14] for GPU simulation. We use HotSpot [30] as a lightweight steady-state thermal evaluator. Per-chiplet power estimates are mapped to fixed floorplan and package-stack models for steady-state thermal evaluation. Each individual design evaluation necessitates approximately 10 minutes of simulation time; consequently, the generation of the entire chiplet dataset consumed a total of 6,144 CPU core-hours. 

Datasets: We randomly sample 2,000 configurations from the 3D chiplet design space and construct 6,000 prompts from exploration histories, each consisting of 10 to 40 evaluated designs. To cover

Each scenario is paired with a reference directional policy derived from the offline dataset. ## 4.2 Comparison with SOTA Methods 

We compare our method with three black-box DSE baselines, XG-Boost [27], BOOM-Explorer [1], and MCT-Explorer [9], and two LLM-based methods, LLAMBO [17] and LLM-DSE [24]. The LLM-based baselines use strong API-based models, including GPT-5.4 and Qwen-235B. All methods are evaluated using five random seeds, with the same 10 initial designs and 30-iteration budget shared across methods for each seed. To account for LLM output stochas-ticity, each LLM-based method is additionally repeated five times per seed, and the resulting HV and ADRS values are averaged. Table 2 shows that our method achieves the best average HV and ADRS on both chiplet design spaces. The 3D task evaluates in-domain policy-guided exploration, whereas the 2.5D task is ex-cluded from fine-tuning and used solely for transfer evaluation. Its strong performance without retraining demonstrates transferabil-ity across related architectures. We observe that pure LLM-driven search may achieve competitive HV but suffer from poor ADRS due to premature concentration on locally promising regions, whereas conventional black-box methods provide broader but less efficient exploration. Our framework combines LLM-generated directional guidance with BO’s uncertainty-aware search, improving both con-vergence and Pareto-front coverage. ## 4.3 Ablation Studies 

We conduct an ablation study on the 3D chiplet dataset to isolate the contributions of adaptive directional policy reasoning and ex-ploration gain-aware fine-tuning. Our full method is compared against standard BO using EHVI without LLM guidance and four LLM-based variants built on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct [22] with P-EHVI. Among them, similarity-based fine-tuning (Sim-FT) adopts GRPO with a reward that measures the similarity between the generated and reference policies. As shown in Table 4, standard BO with EHVI provides a reference for evaluating the overall contribution of LLM-based policy guid-ance. The base LLM offers useful but limited directional guidance, whereas ADPR improves policy generation by structuring bottle-neck analysis, adjustment effect prediction, and feedback reflection. Moreover, exploration gain-aware fine-tuning outperforms Sim-FT by rewarding the potential exploration gain of a policy rather than imitation of a single reference policy, enabling the LLM to generate diverse and effective directional policies. 

## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose an LLM-augmented Bayesian optimiza-tion framework for chiplet DSE. The framework uses adaptive directional policy reasoning to enable the LLM to generate search policies from the current exploration state and simulation feedback, while BO remains responsible for uncertainty-aware candidate se-lection. Exploration gain-aware fine-tuning further aligns policy generation with the Pareto optimization objective. Experiments on 2.5D and 3D chiplet design spaces demonstrate improved ex-ploration efficiency over representative baselines and promising generalization across related architectures. References
